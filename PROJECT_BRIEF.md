# Delay Aware project brief

## Confirmed direction

- Build a flight assistant for travelers taking US domestic flights.
- Start with assistance before booking.
- Begin with flight discovery inside the application, followed by a reliability assessment when the traveler selects an available option.
- Support flight searches for travel dates within the next 30 days in the first version.
- Support nonstop flights and US domestic itineraries with one connection (two flight legs). Itineraries with two or more connections remain outside this expansion. The one-connection flow is implemented; gather any further product decisions one question at a time.
- Support one-way searches only in the first version; round-trip searches are deferred.
- Alternative-flight searches may include the selected travel date, the previous day, and the following day. All candidates must remain within the supported 30-day window; past dates are excluded.
- Alternative flights may use nearby airports within 50 straight-line miles of either originally selected airport. Keep one-way US domestic scope, supporting nonstop and eligible one-connection journeys in the expansion, and clearly identify any changed airport.
- Default alternative recommendations should balance historical reliability with ticket price. Show the underlying fare and reliability evidence so travelers can understand the tradeoff. Numerical scoring weights have not yet been selected or validated.
- Travelers may set an optional maximum ticket budget. Search results and recommendations must respect this limit; an unknown fare cannot be assumed to fit the budget. No price limit is applied when the field is empty. The current search interface accepts a whole-dollar USD maximum for the displayed fare; unquoted fees and ground transport are not included.
- Use a flight search form with a conversational assistant alongside it. Travelers can browse selectable flight results and discuss the selected option with the assistant.
- Run the first version locally on the user's Mac, with a loopback-only browser interface and Ollama running locally. Public hosting is outside the current scope. Flight search and weather calls still require internet access.
- Use local Ollama models instead of training a custom prediction model for the first version.
- Available models confirmed through the local Ollama service: `gpt-oss:20b` and `devstral:24b`.
- Start with free data sources and no paid data subscriptions.
- Selected sources: SerpApi Google Flights results for flight discovery, BTS for historical reliability, FlightAware AeroAPI for selected-departure identity, recent observations and operational estimates, NOAA Aviation Weather Center and NWS for weather, and OurAirports for airport reference data.
- The free search allowance is accepted for initial testing. The user created a SerpApi account and configured the key locally; a live sample flight search has succeeded.
- Use the most recent 24 months of published flight-level history, showing actual observation coverage and missing months.
- Gather product requirements with one question at a time.
- Presentation preference: use one concise traveler-focused flight summary. Prioritize current status, expected local departure/arrival times, past 15+ minute delays, average arrival lateness, cancellations, and a short recent-performance signal. Do not organize the main view or ordinary assistant answers by data provider. Keep source names and matching/calculation details in an optional disclosure. Long-term and recent measurements remain distinct; a unified presentation does not authorize averaging rates or inventing a forecast.
- Current priority: specify and implement one-connection, single-ticket US domestic itineraries alongside existing nonstop options, preserving the historical and recent-flight reporting already implemented.
- Requested historical-report improvement: combine BTS history with FlightAware AeroAPI, including better operating-flight identification and supplemental observations where available. The user has an AeroAPI account and configured its key locally without sharing it in chat. Use the standard flights lookup and conservative local request caps; no subscription, billing change, long-term history endpoint, or Foresight access is authorized. Preserve source labels, verify flight identities, and avoid counting the same operation twice when combining evidence. Continuous live monitoring remains outside the current scope.

## Intended workflow

1. The traveler uses a search form for one-way US domestic journeys departing within the next 30 days, with an assistant alongside it. The form takes departure and arrival airport codes, date, and optional maximum USD fare. All (nonstop and one connection) is the default, with filters for either type. The initial passenger default is one adult in economy.
2. The application retrieves actual flight options from a suitable free source. Coverage and availability must be verified before promising comprehensive search results.
3. The traveler selects a journey and opens its reliability assessment. For a connection, the application checks ticket details on selection and shows both flight histories together, with explicit per-leg data gaps and ticket eligibility.
4. The assessment presents available flight-level history: number of flights observed, number and percentage delayed, typical delay duration, the period covered, and the source update time. Partial history must be described as partial.
5. The assessment explains seasonal and time-of-day patterns where supported by historical data, and weather risks where forecasts cover the travel date. Historical rates, validated probability estimates, and qualitative risk judgments must be labeled distinctly.
6. The assistant searches alternative nonstop and eligible one-connection journeys on the selected date and one day either side, within the supported 30-day window, including airports within 50 straight-line miles of either selected endpoint. Show all returned matching alternatives regardless of the search filter, letting the traveler choose; ticket types are checked on selection. Balanced historical-reliability/price ranking remains a saved improvement; current alternatives sort by quoted fare. Respect the optional maximum budget and explain date, airport, and fare changes. Different dates or airports alone are not evidence of better reliability. Acceptable departure times remain to be decided. Airport-to-airport distance must not be labeled as driving distance or a ground-travel time estimate.
7. The traveler can ask follow-up questions and refine the search through conversation.

The user explicitly wants a delay probability informed by weather and time of year. This is a desired outcome, not a validated existing capability. The first version should calculate historical rates directly and present weather or seasonal risk separately unless a combined probability estimator has been validated. Ollama coordinates tools and explains evidence; its generated numbers are not probability measurements.

The application should calculate historical reliability from data. Model-generated judgments must be distinguished from measured statistics, live facts, and validated predictions. Missing flight, fare, or forecast information must be shown as unavailable rather than invented.

## Data research

- Aviation Weather Center publishes current aviation observations, terminal forecasts, and advisories through a public API: https://aviationweather.gov/data/api/
- FAA National Airspace System status is a candidate for airport disruption information; its integration interface still needs validation: https://nasstatus.faa.gov/
- SerpApi has been selected for flight discovery. The search adapter has been verified on a live Boston-to-Chicago sample search; wider route coverage still requires evaluation.
- Current airport conditions should only inform recommendations for relevant travel times; they cannot establish conditions weeks or months ahead.
- The 30-day search window is not a promise of 30-day weather forecast coverage. When a forecast does not cover the departure date, show available historical and seasonal evidence and explicitly identify the weather forecast as unavailable for that date.

### Free source review on October 23 2026

- SerpApi has been selected for initial search integration. Its pricing page lists 250 searches per month and 50 per hour on the free plan. Its third-party Google Flights results API supports one-way searches, departure dates, and nonstop filtering. It is not an official Google API. The account key is configured and a real sample route has been verified; comprehensive US route coverage has not been established. Sources: https://serpapi.com/pricing and https://serpapi.com/google-flights-api
- BTS publishes downloadable flight-level on-time performance records and flight-number statistics for reporting carriers. Use these for historical analysis, with the reporting lag made visible, rather than as current schedules or availability. Sources: https://www.bts.gov/airline-data-downloads and https://transportation.libanswers.com/faq/154783
- NWS provides free forecasts, alerts, and observations. Its point forecast documentation describes seven-day forecasts, not a 30-day forecast. Source: https://www.weather.gov/documentation/services-web-api
- OurAirports provides public-domain airport reference data, updated nightly, useful for airport search and location lookup. Source: https://ourairports.com/data/
- AirLabs documents limited free schedule fields and a maximum of 50 results per request for free keys. Its schedules endpoint only looks up to 10 hours ahead, so it does not satisfy the 30-day search requirement. Source: https://airlabs.co/docs/schedules
- Aviationstack lists 100 monthly requests for its non-commercial free plan, covering real-time flights. Historical flights, schedules, and future-flight features appear under paid plans. This does not satisfy our full search-and-history requirements for free. Source: https://aviationstack.com/pricing
- OpenSky documents aircraft tracking rather than commercial schedules, flight delay reports, or fares. It is not a substitute for a pre-booking flight search source. Source: https://opensky-network.org/about/faq
